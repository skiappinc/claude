Southern Honey Butter Cornbread

Why You'll Love This Recipe

  • A lightly sweet, tender crumb thanks to browned butter, buttermilk, and honey in the batter
  • A warm honey butter glaze soaks right into the cornbread for extra moisture in every bite
  • Baked in a hot cast iron skillet for golden, slightly crisp edges
  • Simple pantry ingredients and straightforward steps
  • Ready in under an hour, start to finish

What Does It Taste Like?

This cornbread is sweet but not cake-like, with a soft, moist crumb and a rich, buttery flavor from the browned butter. The honey butter glaze adds a final layer of sweetness and moisture that soaks into the top, while the cast iron skillet gives the edges a light golden crispness.

Recipe Overview

  • Prep Time: 15 minutes
  • Cook Time: 25 minutes
  • Total Time: 45 minutes
  • Servings: 8
  • Oven Temperature: 375°F

Ingredients

For the Cornbread

  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) salted butter
  • 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup cornmeal
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 cup buttermilk, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup honey
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup canola or vegetable oil
  • Shortening, for preparing the skillet

For the Honey Butter Glaze

  • 1/4 cup salted butter
  • 1/4 cup honey
  • 2 tablespoons powdered sugar, optional
  • Pinch of salt

Ingredient Notes

  • Browned butter: Cooking the butter until golden and fragrant adds a nutty depth of flavor to the batter.
  • Buttermilk: Gives the crumb tenderness and a slight tang that balances the sweetness.
  • Honey: Used in both the batter and the glaze for a natural sweetness and moist texture.
  • Cast iron skillet: Preheating the skillet helps create golden, slightly crisp edges on the cornbread.

Kitchen Tools You'll Need

  • Cast iron skillet
  • Small saucepan (for browning butter and making the glaze)
  • Large mixing bowl
  • Whisk
  • Toothpick (for testing doneness and poking holes for the glaze)

Substitutions and Variations

These are optional ideas, not part of the original recipe:

  • Unsalted butter can be used in place of salted butter if you prefer to control the salt level, adding a small pinch of extra salt to the batter.
  • A 9x13-inch baking dish can be used instead of a cast iron skillet if that's what you have on hand, though the edges won't be quite as crisp.
  • Skip the powdered sugar in the glaze for a less sweet, more pourable honey butter finish.

How to Make the Recipe

This cornbread comes together by browning the butter, whisking the dry and wet ingredients separately, combining them, and baking the batter in a hot, greased cast iron skillet. While the cornbread is still warm, a honey butter glaze is poured over the top so it soaks into every slice.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 375°F.
  2. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat until it turns golden brown and gives off a nutty aroma. Set aside to cool slightly.
  3. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, cornmeal, baking powder, and granulated sugar.
  4. In another bowl, whisk together the buttermilk, honey, eggs, vanilla extract, oil, and the cooled browned butter until smooth.
  5. Gradually add the wet ingredients to the dry ingredients, stirring until just combined. A few lumps are fine.
  6. Coat a cast iron skillet with shortening and preheat it in the oven for 4 to 5 minutes.
  7. Carefully pour the batter into the hot skillet and spread it evenly.
  8. Bake for 20 to 24 minutes, until golden on top and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with just a few moist crumbs.
  9. Remove the cornbread from the oven and, while it's still warm, poke small holes across the top with a toothpick.
  10. For the glaze, melt the butter and honey together in a saucepan over low heat. Stir in the powdered sugar, if using, and a pinch of salt until smooth.
  11. Pour the glaze evenly over the warm cornbread, letting it soak into the holes.
  12. Serve warm.

Tips for Success

  • Don't overmix the batter once the wet and dry ingredients are combined, as overmixing can make the cornbread dense.
  • Let the browned butter cool slightly before adding it to the wet ingredients so it doesn't cook the eggs.
  • Preheating the skillet before adding the batter helps give the cornbread golden, slightly crisp edges.

How to Know It's Done

The cornbread is done when the top is golden and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with just a few moist crumbs, after about 20 to 24 minutes of baking.

Storage Instructions

  • Room Temperature: Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days.
  • Refrigerator: Store tightly covered for up to 5 days.

Freezing Instructions

Wrap individual slices well and freeze for up to 3 months.

Common Mistakes

  • Overmixing the batter, which can lead to a tougher, denser cornbread.
  • Skipping the skillet preheat, which results in less golden, crisp edges.
  • Pouring the glaze on cornbread that has cooled completely, which prevents it from soaking in as well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use unsalted butter instead of salted?
Yes, unsalted butter works as a substitution; just add a small pinch of extra salt to the batter to balance the flavor.

Can I bake this in a baking dish instead of a skillet?
A 9x13-inch baking dish can be used instead of a cast iron skillet, though the edges will be softer rather than crisp.

Can I freeze this cornbread?
Yes, wrap individual slices well and freeze for up to 3 months.

Why did my cornbread turn out dense?
Overmixing the batter after combining the wet and dry ingredients is the most common cause of a dense crumb.

Conclusion
